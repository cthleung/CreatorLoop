#### [SEOYUN'S POV]

"Seoddaeng-ah, how many times are you gonna play this?" Haeun asked, laughing, with her seat pulled so far forward that her chin was practically resting on the steering wheel. "It's been this one song the whole way up the mountain."

Seoyun had her own seat tipped back as far as it would go and her sneakers up on the dash, so the only road she had was a patch of fog between her knees. She kept singing under her breath instead of answering, because the song was good, and Haeun always complained about her music right before she caved and sang along.

"Because it's GOOD, unnie. Just listen to this part -"

She went for the high note and missed it by an octave, grinning the whole way up, and Haeun laughed so hard she had to check her mirror twice.

The Weekend House crew had stuck a camera to the windshield before they left the guesthouse, and its red light blinked at Seoyun like it expected a show. She gave it her best face, lip curled up on one side and an eyebrow hiked up to her hairline, then reached up and tilted it at the windshield, where there was nothing to film but fog. Every few bends the fog tore open on a field of canola so yellow it looked fake, then closed up again.

"None of this is going in the final cut," she told it.

"*Ya*, the PD's gonna kill you."

"Then the PD can come up here and fight me."

The eggs were why they were out here at all. A crate of them sat on the back seat, wedged in next to the toilet paper and a box of hallabong for the guests arriving tomorrow, and Haeun was taking every hairpin so slowly for their sake that Seoyun could have gotten out and walked beside the car.

"You're such a mom," Seoyun said. "You were like this at the awards too. Sending LUMEN juice."

"That was MANNERS."

Seoyun had already opened the hallabong. "It's for the guests," she said, digging her thumb in so the peel sprayed oil across the dash. "I'm testing it for the guests. Quality control. Open."

Haeun opened her mouth without taking her eyes off the road, and Seoyun fed her a piece. Haeun chewed, and her shoulders came down from around her ears. "Okay. That's really good."

"Right? I told you."

Haeun had bought her hat the last time they were here, back in November, a knitted tangerine with a felt leaf sprouting out of the top, and she'd worn it every day of this trip too. The leaf flopped into her eyes whenever she checked the mirror, and she pushed it back without looking.

"You weren't even with us for half of last time," Seoyun said, flicking a bit of peel at her. "You went off on your own day and ditched us. Was it everything you dreamed of?"

Haeun smiled at the road. "It was really nice, actually. I'd always wanted to do that, just go somewhere by myself and -"

Seoyun reached over and turned the song up.

Haeun laughed and turned it back down. "*Ya*, let me finish."

"Tell me later."

The heater only reached the middle of the windshield, so the side windows had fogged over at the edges. While the song came back around to the start, Seoyun drew on her side of the glass with one finger, a little house with a pointy roof and lumpy tangerine trees all around it. When they stopped at a junction to let nobody past, Haeun glanced over at it, then back at the road.

Seoyun's Instax was in the cupholder, the sky-blue one she took everywhere. She picked it up and caught Haeun in it with both hands at ten and two and the tangerine hat on. The flash went off across the glass, and Haeun yelped.

"*Ya*, don't, I look ugly -"

The print slid out with a whir, and Seoyun laid it face up on the dash to come up on its own, because people who shook them were animals. Then she held the camera out across the console.

"Here. Keep it."

"What? No. It's yours."

"I've got other cameras."

"It's such a hassle, Seoddaeng. I'll have to carry it around everywhere and -"

"It's a hassle now," Seoyun said. "Later it's treasure."

Haeun opened her mouth to argue, so Seoyun hooked the strap over her head while both of Haeun's hands were busy on the wheel and there was nothing she could do about it but shriek. "*Ya*, I'm DRIVING -" The camera bumped against her chest and knocked the hat down over one eye, and they laughed too hard to talk until Haeun gave up and let it hang there.

Past the next bend, Haeun went back to narrating her driving under her breath. "Okay, left. Okay. Slow, slow..." Seoyun whispered along a word behind her until Haeun swatted her knee.

"You're actually really good at this now," Seoyun said.

"What do you mean, NOW? I've always been good at driving."

"Unnie. The U-turn lane."

"Aaah, why would you bring that up -" Haeun groaned, laughing. "I KNOW, okay? Why did I even do that? I still don't know why."

Seoyun let her head drop back against the headrest, shut her eyes and let her mouth hang open, the exact face the instructor had made when Haeun parked them in a lane she had no business being in, and Haeun laughed so hard she had to slow down.

"Stop it, stop, I'm gonna crash -" Once she'd caught her breath, she added, "And feet off the dash. If we crash, your knees go through your face."

Seoyun's feet stayed where they were.

"Okay, okay," Haeun said. "Keep your eyes closed."

"Why? Are you gonna do something weird?"

"No! Just keep them closed. Just play with me, okay?"

Seoyun kept them closed. The car leaned into a hairpin with the eggs rattling on the back seat, and the indicator started ticking for a turn on a mountain with nobody on it to tell.

"What do you sm-"

"Tangerines."

"You're HOLDING one, you cheater."

"It still counts, it's in the car."

"You're so obsessed with them. You've been like this since last time."

Seoyun grinned with her eyes still shut. "They remind me of you."

"What, because of my hat?"

"No... it's like, if you eat too many, your hands go all yellow, right? And then it doesn't come off for days." She laughed. "That's you."

Haeun didn't say anything back. The wipers squeaked across the glass and back while the song started over, and Seoyun waited for the joke back that didn't come. Then Haeun started singing along under her breath, the meadow line, from the song she'd been complaining about since the bottom of the mountain. Seoyun kept her eyes shut so she wouldn't have to tease her for it.

"Your turn," she said instead. "Close your eyes."

"Seoddaeng-ah, I'm driving."

"Just for a second, unnie, one second, I'll watch the road for you, I promise -"

"Your eyes are CLOSED, Seoddaeng."

The car swung toward the middle of the road and back as Haeun cracked up, and the eggs rattled behind them. "If I close my eyes, we're both going off this mountain, and then who's gonna sing our parts?"

"Okay. What else do you smell?"

Seoyun pulled in a long breath through her nose. There was hallabong on her fingers, and under it, salt.

"...The ocean."

"Seoddaeng, we're in the middle of the mountains."

"I know. I can still smell it, though."

The fan roared up as Haeun put the defrost on, and warm air poured past Seoyun's face. Then Haeun turned the music down herself.

"You know where I went last time? On my day by myself?" Haeun said. "I drove up here. This road. And the whole time I kept seeing all this stuff and thinking, Seoddaeng would love this."

The song came round to the meadow again, quieter now.

*Jeo pureun chowon wie geurim gateun jibeul jitgo -*

"Should we come build a house in the mountains at Jeju-do and live here later?"

Seoyun opened her eyes. The defrost had eaten her drawing in from the edges, so only the roof of the little house was left on the glass, and past it the fog had thinned enough to show black stone walls going by. Her own face sat in the window over all of it, and past her shoulder was Haeun's, with the hat slipping over one eye and the camera strap across her chest.

Seoyun pressed her forehead to the cold glass beside what was left of the house. "*Mm*."

"Is that a yes?"

"*Mm*."

Haeun laughed, and Seoyun shut her eyes again, smiling into the window.

Tangerines. Ocean. Rock. Ocean. Tangerines.
