## Round 1: the checklist and the research (draft 2)

Draft 2 worked through the review notes in `05-review-notes.md` and pulled in real details from the reference footage transcripts I'd found. My direction:

> Creative license allowed. What's already in the [novel] remains canon. [...] This scene is more about establishing this texture without going too deep into it, blending reality and fiction within our constraints.

> Also, 'casual as suggesting lunch' type voice-quality similes are banned too. I'd rather just use an adverb.

That second line became a permanent rule for every chapter after this one.

> Okay, work that into the draft. Then review your own prose, especially the dialogue, where it could feel scripted or stiff, on the second pass.

> Try to do the Tom and Jerry bickering without the characters repeating each other, so be creative about it. Like, a little more cracky. None of that "I hate you" / "No you don't" / "No, I don't" style nonsense that we got rid of in [an earlier chapter].

The bickering rule went permanent too: every comeback moves sideways and gets crackier, and nobody repeats the other's line back.

**What the AI changed:**
- Every "unnie" is gone.
- The non-events and invented numbers are fixed or turned into actions. "Seoyun's feet stayed where they were" became "Seoyun opened one eye and crossed her ankles on the dash."
- Real details from the footage went in as props, 1 clause each: the canola hairpin under Haeun's hat, the dried squid in the cupholder, Haeun calling herself a preschooler, the music box.

**The AI's self-review, 2 passes:**
- **Pass 1, the prose.** "Both hands" came up 3 times, so one became "her hands were busy on the wheel." "Fog" was everywhere, so the plain uses became "the white," and the jokes kept the word (Haeun stops "to let the fog go first").

## What became permanent

These rulings left this scene and went into the style guide, the scanner and its tests, so they apply to every scene after it:
- **Narrated non-events:** state what is, unless the non-event is the beat itself.
- **Invented numbers, figurative ones included:** drop them, or pick them with the dice roller.
- **Delivery similes** ("casual as suggesting lunch") are banned. Use an adverb.
- **Tom and Jerry bickering:** every comeback moves sideways, and nobody repeats the other's line.
- **Tags follow what the narrator can perceive:** sight with her eyes open, sound and touch with them shut.
- **The author's own designs beat the rules:** the staccato palindrome stays, because it's on purpose.
- **"Something about,"** used like people say it out loud, is fine.
